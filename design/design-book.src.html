The concept

A working tool before it is a costume. The kit gives Bureau its character, but every flourish has a job: a seal is a grade, the sash marks what is live, brass marks where you are. When style and clarity disagree, clarity wins.

In Renaissance Rome, a master assassin ran a hidden bureau: scouts went out across the city, contracts came in, each one was studied, prepared for, and struck with precision, often without the master lifting a finger. Bureau runs your job hunt the same way.

The visual language is drawn from that era's most recognisable kit: the master's cream hooded robe, the crimson sash and cape lining, the oiled leather of bracers and belts, the brass of buckles and insignia, the steel of a concealed blade, and the parchment pages of a polymath inventor's codex. Bureau's insignia, palette and type are our own; nothing is borrowed from any game's trademarks.

Motion and access

Motion

  • One easing curve, 140 to 220 ms, none of it required to read the page.
  • One authored moment: the sash drawing across when a campaign goes live. A thin moving line shows a round in progress. Nothing bounces.
  • Reduced-motion settings turn all of it off.

Access

  • Text at least 4.5:1 and focus rings at least 3:1 in both themes, inside the rail and on the sash.
  • Brass focus ring on every control, drawn inward on rows inside clipped sheets.
  • Touch targets of at least 44 px, with labelled roles on tabs, switches, dialogs and live regions.
  • States never rely on colour alone.

Voice

Speaks like a mentor, not a mascot.

Plain first

Navigation and buttons use ordinary words: Jobs, Studio, Dispatch, "Draft application". The Renaissance flavour lives in the look and the empty states, never in a control you need to understand.

Honest always

Bureau never inflates. Scores come with their working. A missing must-have is shown as missing. The fact lock calls out invented claims, even in our own drafts.

Brief and warm

Short sentences, active voice, no exclamation marks, no hype. A good line reads like a trusted colleague: "4 strong matches await your eye."

Applications sound like you

Claude reads your CV once and writes down how you write. Every letter follows that voice plus your tone setting. The house bans: "passionate", "fast-paced", "team player", "leverage", "synergy", "I am writing to apply", flattery, and em-dash flourishes.

Every number and employer in a draft must appear in your CV, or the fact lock flags it.

The hunt

One campaign band on the Overview. Each round it scouts, has Claude weigh what's new, tailors a CV and letter for every role above the bar, and applies by email or through the company's own form in a real browser.

What it will and won't do

  • Applies through employers' own logged-out forms (Greenhouse, Lever, Ashby, Workable…) and by email. It never logs in to LinkedIn or Indeed.
  • Claude answers each form from your profile and standing answers. Demographic questions get "decline to self-identify".
  • Never solves a CAPTCHA and never guesses. Those roles go to Needs you with the form filled; "Finish in browser" opens it for your click.
  • Only sends when the fact lock is clean. One automatic rewrite, then it stops and holds the draft for you.
  • Daily cap, a stop button, and Rehearsal mode (fills forms, keeps screenshots, doesn't submit).

The engine

Every step, in order. Linen steps are code, tinted steps are Claude, the brass step is you.

iScout16 public sources: ATS boards, aggregators, HN, Reddit RSS, game-dev feeds. Plus a Claude web deep scan.
iiGateFree filters: is it tech, which discipline, dedupe by URL and fingerprint, freshness, deal-breakers.
iiiPre-scoreDiscipline, title, skills overlap, logistics, recency, seniority. No tokens spent.
ivAssessHaiku 5.5. Pass 1: requirements from the posting alone. Pass 2: matched against the CV, with proof.
vTailorLetter, outreach note, ATS-safe CV, in your voice, from your facts.
viFact lockCode checks every figure and employer; a fresh auditor lists unsupported claims. One rewrite, or it holds.
viiApplyEmail from your inbox, or the company form in a real browser: fields answered, CV uploaded, submitted.
viiiNeeds youCAPTCHAs and questions only you can answer, pre-filled. One click finishes them.

The score is computed in code

requirements = Σ weight × match / Σ weight weight: critical 3 · important 2 · nice 1 match: met 1 · partial ½ · missing 0 dimensions = skills .30 + experience .20 + domain .15 + logistics .15 + pay .10 + growth .10 score = .45 × requirements + .35 × dimensions + .20 × pre-filter caps: stated must-have missing → ≤ 64 · dealbreaker → ≤ 40 low legitimacy −25 · under-levelled −8

Why it's built this way

  • Claude judges; code weighs. The same assessment always gives the same number, and the weights can be tuned.
  • The free gate cuts thousands of listings to the few dozen worth tokens. Prompt caching keeps the rubric and profile warm across a whole scan.
  • Job postings are treated as untrusted data. Injection attempts are flagged as red flags, never obeyed.
  • Haiku 5.5 scores in volume; Opus 5.5 writes everything a hiring manager reads. Server-side fallback on refusals, a model per task, and a monthly budget cap.

Security

Built to leave localhost safely.

i
Loopback by defaultListens on 127.0.0.1 only. It refuses to bind elsewhere unless a passphrase is set.
ii
Host, Origin & path guardUnknown Host headers are refused (defeats DNS rebinding), cross-site requests rejected, and every request guarded on its decoded path, so encoded URLs can't slip past.
iii
Session + CSRF256-bit HttpOnly, SameSite=Strict cookie; every change needs a per-session token a hostile page can't read.
iv
Passphrase lockscrypt (N=2¹⁵) hashed, rate-limited, session rotated on login.
v
Secrets vaultAPI key and SMTP password encrypted with AES-256-GCM; the master key is sealed with Windows DPAPI. Secrets are never sent to the browser and are redacted from logs.
vi
SSRF guardAll outbound fetches validate every resolved IP at connect time, re-check each redirect, and block private, loopback, link-local and metadata ranges.
vii
Hostile contentJob HTML sanitised on the server (sanitize-html) and again in the browser (DOMPurify), under a strict CSP: no inline scripts, no third-party origins.
viii
Uploads10 MB cap, type checked by magic bytes, stored under random names outside the web root.
ix
Prompt-injection posturePostings are fenced as data and can't forge the prompt's tags. An apply address is trusted only if it appears in that job's own text. The fact lock and the daily cap bound what the hunt can send.
x
The hunt's browserEvery request it makes passes the same private-network guard. Fresh context per application, downloads off. It never logs in to LinkedIn or Indeed, so your accounts can't be banned for it.
